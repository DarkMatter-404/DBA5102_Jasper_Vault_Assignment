01 · The problem00:00–00:30

Pricing protection while keeping it attractive

Perpetual Protect gives traders short-duration protection on their positions. Customers pay a premium upfront, and Jasper pays eligible claims. A useful pricing model must balance expected claims with customers’ willingness to buy.

Our question: can structured market data and narrative information improve that balance?

Suggested speaking script · 30 seconds

Our proposal is a combined pricing framework for Jasper Vault's short-duration PP protection. A customer pays a premium and Jasper pays eligible claims. The challenge is to cover that risk while keeping the price attractive enough to buy. We combine duration, BTC market conditions, news and social sentiment, then limit the final adjustment. We will explain the model and evaluate it against all six assignment requirements.

02 · Proposed solution00:30–01:30

Four information layers, one bounded price

PP quote = compatible reference premium × pricing multiplier

LayerWhat it contributes
DurationA starting estimate of claims relative to premiums.
Market conditionsRecent volatility, momentum, time and option direction.
News NLPRecent headline activity and text patterns.
Social sentimentBTC discussion activity, sentiment and data availability.

A regularized statistical regression estimates expected payouts. News text becomes numerical features through TF-IDF and three SVD components. The social provider supplies sentiment scores. A separate pricing rule shrinks the risk adjustment towards the reference price and applies the price limit.

The model uses a reference compatible with recorded PP premiums. Jasper must confirm how this maps to its official base price before scoring a multiplier.

Suggested speaking script · 60 seconds

Think of this as a small adjustment to a compatible existing PP quote. First, duration sets a starting estimate of claims relative to premiums. The model then checks market conditions, recent headlines and available social sentiment. These inputs alter the estimated payout risk. Finally, a separate pricing rule converts the risk score into a small increase or discount. The technical model is a regularized regression for expected payouts, with a log link and a fixed starting estimate. News text becomes numeric features through TF-IDF and three SVD components. The social dataset already supplies its sentiment scores. We do not ask a language model to set a price. One important implementation detail is that our reference must match the recorded-premium definition used for calibration. Jasper must confirm that mapping before official base-price scoring.

Within this scope, the model allows risk to vary with volatility, momentum, time, direction and the seven protection durations from 0.5 to 24 hours. It uses completed candles and past information.

The assignment data does not establish performance across different strike distances. An OTM extension requires verified moneyness and relevant contracts.

Suggested speaking script · 50 seconds

The assignment asks us to account for market conditions and moneyness. The class notes are important here: all assignment contracts are at the money, with zero deductible and full coverage. At the money means the strike equals the contract's entry reference price. So we should not invent a comparison between at-the-money and out-of-the-money products from differences against an external exchange candle. Within this ATM scope, risk still changes with volatility, momentum, time, option direction and duration. The seven durations range from half an hour to twenty-four hours. We use completed market candles and past information only. Testing other strike distances would require contracts with verified moneyness and outcomes.

09 · Criterion 607:55–08:55

Use the framework for a future PP quote

0 LLM calls

Needed in the pricing calculation

  1. Collect current information. Record completed market candles, headlines and social observations, including their arrival times.
  2. Check availability. Use only information available before the quote and within the freshness limits.
  3. Calculate the quote. Apply saved model parameters and the price limit. Use explicit fallbacks when inputs are missing.
  4. Measure the outcome. Record the offered price, the purchase decision and any payout.

Real-world evaluation needs continuous feeds, a confirmed reference price and actual conversion data. It must also assess hedging, fees, capital and tail losses. The weekly public social sample demonstrates integration.

Suggested speaking script · 60 seconds

For tomorrow's quote, the system first collects current completed candles, headlines and social observations, recording when each actually arrives. At quote time it uses only fresh information that was already available. Saved coefficients calculate the expected-risk adjustment and the pricing rule applies the cap. If social data is missing, that layer becomes neutral. If market inputs are missing or stale, the system uses the duration fallback. Invalid contract timing causes an abstention. The system can then log the offered price, the customer's decision and any payout, which are needed for a meaningful commercial evaluation. There is no LLM call in the pricing path. The current public weekly social sample is suitable for demonstration, while live use needs a timely feed, verified reference-price semantics and an evaluation that includes fees, hedging, capital and tail risk.
