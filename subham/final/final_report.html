Jasper Vault Perpetual Protect: the team's final pricing solution¶

Summary¶

  • Decision. For each of the 4,155 validation orders, we submit Maksim's frozen premium: fair value (the expected payout) × 1.25, never below JV's base price. File: outputs/final_submission.csv, column recommended_premium_USDC, total 205,824 USDC.
  • Why this model. It is the only team model that meets all six JV criteria (section 2). In July 2026, priced by a model fitted only on earlier months, it made 22,187 USDC more than JV's actual prices with no demand response (90% interval +15,472 to +29,549). Under the conservative demand rule it made 10,782 USDC more (+3,506 to +18,719).
  • Better, not only higher. 58% of training orders get a lower price than JV charged (66% in July). On the 544 August orders where JV's own price is visible, our price is 0.957 × JV's price.
  • Improvement we tested. Chris's order-flow loading adds profit over the model, but a flat raise of the same size does as well. The rule we committed before scoring therefore keeps it out of the main price. The file carries it as an option (section 5).
  • Risk. A cap of 250,000 USDC of open protection per direction cut the worst training day from -8,399 to -6,594 USDC, for 11% of profit. On the validation orders it would pause 9.4% of orders (30% of premium).
  • Limits. JV's demand rule is unknown. In August 2026 (BTC +25%) the model lost 12,667 USDC, and JV's own prices also lost money where we can see them. Section 12 lists every limit.

1. The idea in one picture¶

Your plan and Maksim's model are the same idea: model the payout, then add a margin. It is the lecturer's formula "choose the price P that maximises a(P, x) × [P − μ(x) − c(x)]", with μ(x) = expected payout = fair value.

Step What happens Example: 16 Jun 2026, 09:39 New York time, 1-hour put, 0.1702 BTC
1. Expected move A Poisson regression forecasts the size of the BTC move in the window from recent volatility (1 h, 4 h, 1 day, 1 week), the time of day, and US releases (CPI, jobs report, FOMC) 0.66% (the busiest hour of the day)
2. Fair value Black-Scholes turns the expected move into the expected payout per BTC 217.85 USDC per BTC
3. Margin × 1.25 (a 20% margin on premium; target loss ratio 0.80) 272.31 USDC per BTC
4. Floor Never below JV's base price, when the order has one Base 92.60 per BTC: the floor does not apply
5. Premium × order size 46.35 USDC (JV charged 28.23; the payout was 84.07)

Both prices lost money on this one order. The model is judged on thousands of orders, not on one.

Two layers sit on top of the price. They do not change the main column:

  • Order-flow loading (option): up to +30% on orders that go against the crowded side of JV's open book.
  • Exposure cap (risk rule): pause new sales in one direction above 250,000 USDC of open protection.

3. Profit on training data¶

Each month is priced by a model fitted only on earlier data. JV's demand rule is unknown, so we show seven rules. "None" keeps every order. "Conservative" lets a higher price lose orders and a lower price win none. "Keep if ≤ …" keeps an order only if our price is at most that multiple of JV's price.

4. Better pricing, not only higher pricing¶

JV rejects "raise every price". Three facts show that the model moves prices to where the risk is:

  1. Most orders get cheaper. 58% of training orders are priced below what JV charged. The model raises the price where JV lost money (4-hour and 8-hour products, orders between 08:00 and 12:00 UTC, release windows) and cuts it where JV earned a lot (30-minute products, orders between 00:00 and 04:00 UTC).
  2. The level in training is higher, but the level JV charges moved. In training the model charges 1.166 × JV (February to June) and 1.206 × JV (July). In August, JV's own multiplier rose to about 1.53 × base. On the 544 August orders where JV's price is visible, the model is 0.957 × JV's price:
Product Orders Our price ÷ JV's price

  1. The model ranks risk. The ordered-Lorenz Gini measures whether the orders the model marks up are the ones that pay out more. 0 means no ranking power. It is 0.244 (90% interval 0.160 to 0.314) for February to June and 0.097 (0.041 to 0.148) for July.

An honest limit: under the conservative demand rule, a flat raise of JV's prices to the same total does about as well as the model in training (Maksim's README, section 5.1). The model's case rests on what a flat raise cannot do: it needs no JV price (JV's price is unknown for 3,611 of the 4,155 validation orders), it ranks risk, and it cuts the worst days.

5. The improvement we tested: Chris's order-flow loading¶

The signal. Traders often buy protection after BTC has already moved, all on the same side. JV's open book then leans to one side, and the move partly reverses. Orders that join the crowded side pay out less than their premium; orders that oppose it pay out more (training, book built from expiry dates only, never from closedAt):

Order vs JV's open book Orders Loss ratio at JV's price Payout ÷ fair value
book under 1 BTC 3,676 0.81 0.94
joins the crowd 898 0.69 0.77
neutral 1,968 1.11 1.08
opposes the crowd 608 1.12 1.12

The layer. Premium × (1 + 0.3 × how strongly the order opposes the crowd), only when the book holds at least 1 BTC. It never cuts a price. In July it loads 31% of orders by 6.1% on average.

The test. Before any scoring, we committed a rule (README.md, commit cd9437f). The loading enters the main price only if it beats the model in both periods and also beats a flat raise of the same size in July under the central demand rule. The last condition separates "better" from "higher".

Condition Result
1. July: loading beats the model passed: +1,596 USDC (90% interval +692 to +2,610), conservative e = 2

Decision: the loading stays out of the main price. We cannot show that its gain comes from better allocation rather than from the higher price level. The file keeps it as an option: premium_frozen_model_USDC × orderflow_loading. With it, the validation total would be 211,553 USDC (+2.8%), still 0.978 × JV's revealed August price. The signal itself is strongest as a risk signal: it explains why the exposure cap is needed (section 6).

6. Risk: cap the open exposure, do not just raise the price¶

JV's gain on an order is capped at the premium; its loss is not. Losses come in a few days when BTC trends and the crowd is right. No price set at the time of the order can foresee those days. The model already lowers the worst day (training, February to July):

8. Market conditions and moneyness¶

Market conditions enter through the expected move: recent volatility over four horizons, the time of day in New York time (variance after the 09:30 equity open is 3.8 × the weekly average), and scheduled US releases. JV's base price ignores the time of day.

Moneyness. Every order in the data is at the money, so no outcome can test other strikes. The pricer handles them by construction: the same Black-Scholes formula prices a strike away from spot, with the same 25% margin. Protection with a short duration loses its value fastest when the strike moves away:

Protection length At the money 0.25% out of the money 0.50% out of the money 1.00% out of the money 2.00% out of the money
30 min 100% 18% 1% 0% 0%
1 h 100% 51% 22% 2% 0%
2 h 100% 58% 30% 6% 0%
4 h 100% 66% 41% 13% 1%
8 h 100% 72% 49% 20% 2%
12 h 100% 75% 54% 26% 4%
24 h 100% 82% 67% 43% 14%

So the case's question "should ATM and OTM protection use the same markup?" has a clear answer here: the same margin on fair value, because the fair value already falls with distance and with shorter protection.

9. The margin is a business choice¶

Under the smooth and conservative demand rules, a higher margin always looks more profitable in training. That is exactly the "just raise prices" trap JV warns about: our data holds only orders that were bought, so it cannot measure how many traders a higher price drives away. Maksim set 25% before any test at that margin. We keep it. (February to July, walk-forward, gross profit in USDC.)

Margin on fair value No demand response Conservative e = 1 Keep if not dearer Orders kept (cons. e = 1) JV premium kept (cons. e = 1)
10% 28,758 25,213 12,071 93% 89%
20% 51,383 41,974 13,544 91% 85%
25% 62,879 49,790 15,401 90% 84%
30% 74,434 57,198 16,104 88% 82%
40% 97,665 70,794 15,471 86% 78%

A 25% margin is also the best margin for a seller whose buyers have a constant price elasticity of 5 (Lerner rule: P = cost × e ÷ (e − 1) = 1.25 × cost). JV can measure its real elasticity with a small randomised price test, for example ±10% on a random 10% of quotes.

10. Practical use¶

  • Inputs: 1-minute BTC candles, a public calendar of US releases, and (for the cap and the loading) JV's own open book. No order outcome enters the model.
  • One setting: the margin. The base-price floor and the cap are simple rules.
  • Deployment: the model runs off-chain from an oracle price feed and publishes a quote; the cap is a counter per direction (Blockchain lectures 6–7: off-chain model, oracle, limits).
  • Reproducible: final_solution.ipynb rebuilds outputs/ from the team's saved files; tests/ holds 28 automatic checks, including one that changes every closedAt value and gets the same prices.

12. Limitations¶

  1. Demand was not estimated. All demand rules are assumptions; JV's rule is unknown. Under "keep only if not dearer than JV" the model does not beat JV in July.
  2. Trending months can lose at any price. August 2026 lost money for the model and for JV's own prices. The cap limits such months; no price set at the time of the order prevents them.
  3. Data disclosure. The validation file still contains closedAt, and its candles run to 14 October, so the hidden outcomes can be rebuilt. No model used them as an input; the team ran one agreed aggregate check after freezing the prices and changed no price afterwards. JV may want to blank closedAt in future releases.
  4. Gross underwriting profit only. Fees, funding and capital costs are not included.
  5. Small segments. 12-hour, 24-hour and release-window orders are tens to a few hundred orders. A handful of days and about 1% of orders drive monthly profit.
